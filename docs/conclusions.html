Conclusions

This project demonstrated that machine learning can effectively predict Counter-Strike 2 round outcomes with 86.65% accuracy when provided with appropriate game state features. The journey from initial models that performed no better than random guessing to a final model that correctly predicts five out of six rounds illustrates the critical importance of understanding what to measure. The most significant finding is that economic advantage—how much money teams have invested in weapons and armor—dominates all other factors in determining who wins a round. This insight, while intuitive to experienced players, is now quantified: equipment advantage alone accounts for nearly 30% of the predictive signal. For competitive players and coaches, this reinforces that strategic economic decisions about when to save, when to force buy, and when to invest in full equipment matter more than raw mechanical skill in determining match outcomes.

The analysis also revealed that professional CS2 players naturally cluster into two distinct roles based on their statistical patterns. Entry fraggers take aggressive positions, secure opening kills, and accept higher death counts in exchange for creating opportunities. Support players survive longer, secure kills in post-plant situations, and provide stability for their teams. Principal component analysis confirmed that just two dimensions capture over 70% of the variation in player behavior, suggesting that despite the apparent complexity of professional gameplay, player performance can be meaningfully summarized along simple axes of activity level and aggression. These findings could inform team composition decisions, player development strategies, and performance evaluation metrics for esports organizations.

Looking forward, this project establishes a foundation for more sophisticated esports analytics. The techniques developed here—parsing game demos, engineering predictive features, and applying ensemble machine learning methods—could be extended to other competitive games with similar data availability. The 86.65% accuracy achieved suggests that real-time prediction systems for live matches are feasible, potentially providing coaches with strategic insights during tactical timeouts or analysts with probability estimates for broadcast commentary. While challenges remain in scaling data collection and incorporating positional analysis, this work demonstrates that competitive gaming generates rich data that, when properly processed, yields actionable insights about what separates winning teams from losing ones.
