Introduction

Counter Strike Global Offensive 2 is a competitive 5 v 5 first person shooter game. Played by an average of 600,000 people per day and over 30 million unique users, the game is very popular in Europe and South America, but recently has been gaining popularity in the United States as well. The game is played professionally with many different organizations and teams at different skill levels. The game is very tactical and simple, and there are thousands of full game demos that can be parsed into game info for machine learning. Many sports betting sites exist to bet on counter strike pro games and many even feature live betting features. I am curious if I would be able to make competitive models for sports betting on games. Counter strike has a very high skill ceiling and I am also curious how machine learning can help me improve, as well as what insights I could gain about positions and gameplay that I otherwise would not have gotten. I enjoy how competitive CS2 is and I am interested in optimizing the performance of players using AI. I think that the amount of data will provide insights if combined with machine learning.

Due to its popularity many sports betting platforms likely use machine learning algorithms to produce their odds. There has also been lots of work developing api's to scrape game data from different platforms so I hope to use these resources. Python has many libraries that can parse game data and I will have a lot of resources of past betting odds in order to evaluate my predictions. I want to do more comprehensive research into what skills can be the most impactful for players and how you could get better at them.

This project applies multiple supervised learning approaches to predict round winners in professional CS2 matches. I implemented and compared decision trees, naive bayes classifiers, and ensemble boosting methods including AdaBoost, Gradient Boosting, and XGBoost. Each algorithm offers different strengths in handling the complex, high dimensional feature space extracted from game demos. The supervised learning framework requires labeled data where round outcomes are known, allowing models to learn patterns that distinguish winning rounds from losing ones.

Through extensive feature engineering and model experimentation, I discovered that economy is the dominant factor in determining round outcomes. Equipment advantage consistently emerged as the strongest predictor across all algorithms, with XGBoost achieving 67.12 percent accuracy when using state based features that capture team equipment values, player counts, and health at multiple time points throughout each round. This finding quantitatively confirms what experienced players understand intuitively that teams with superior weapon loadouts have significantly higher win probabilities. The 8.42 percentage point improvement over baseline models demonstrates that what you measure matters more than which algorithm you choose.

The methodology evolved from simple outcome based features like total kills and damage to sophisticated time series sampling that extracts game state at ten different time points per round. This approach captures temporal dynamics showing how equipment advantages, player counts, and tactical positioning interact over time to determine outcomes. By parsing professional match demos using the demoparser2 library, I built a dataset of 288 samples from multiple matches with 28 engineered features spanning economy, bomb state, time phase, player health, and weapon counts. The analysis reveals actionable insights for competitive players emphasizing that winning the economy war through strategic saving and forcing opponent eco rounds is more controllable and impactful than relying solely on mechanical skill execution.
